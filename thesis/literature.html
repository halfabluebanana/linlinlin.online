QMSS master's thesis · literature

Four literatures, and the gap between them

What is established, what is contested, and what is left.

The gap has narrowed twice during this review and is stated here at its current, defensible width. An earlier version of this project claimed no published study runs a disconnected baseline. That is no longer true, and the claim has been corrected rather than defended.

1 · Identification

Why peer effects cannot be recovered from observational network data, and why that stops being true in an agent system.

Manski (1993), Review of Economic Studies 60:3
The reflection problem. Observed similarity within a group bundles endogenous effects (you respond to peers' behaviour), contextual effects (you respond to their characteristics) and correlated effects (you share traits or an environment). Not separately identified. The thesis is this problem with the correlated term made directly observable: the base model is the shared trait, and severing the channel measures it.
Shalizi & Thomas (2011), Sociological Methods & Research
Homophily and contagion are generically confounded — an impossibility result, not a data limitation. Latent traits drive both tie formation and outcome, so diffusion estimates are unidentified without strong assumptions.
Aral, Muchnik & Sundararajan (2009), PNAS 106:51
What separating the two actually costs in real network data: a large matched-sample apparatus. The contrast that shows why an experimental version is worth building.
Cohen-Cole & Fletcher (2008), BMJ 337
The cautionary case — the same methods find "contagion" in acne, height and headaches. Cite when arguing why a measured baseline matters.
Kleinberg & Raghavan (2021), PNAS 118:22
Algorithmic monoculture. The substantive reading of the baseline-homogeneity term: correlated outcomes from a shared model. If most apparent collective behaviour is disposition rather than interaction, that is a monoculture finding with direct consequences for systems deploying many instances of one model.

2 · Agent systems as a setting

Why the confound is escapable here and nowhere else.

Chang et al. (ICWSM 2025) · Papachristou & Yuan (PNAS Nexus 2025)
Both ask whether LLM agent networks reproduce human network structure. The design lever they establish is the one this thesis uses: wiring is assignable. Set which agent can talk to whom, and they remain unconnected unless wired.

The move: in human data, ties and traits co-vary and cannot be pulled apart. In an agent population the model is held fixed and the channel is a switch, so what is unidentified observationally becomes an experimental contrast.

3 · The nearest prior this is the contested one

Where the gap actually sits, after correction.

Kong, Lai, Piao & Evans (2026), arXiv:2605.17193
Multi-LLM systems exhibit robust semantic collapse: systematic convergence in semantic representation despite apparent lexical variation, across 10 models and 200–1,000 rounds, with thirteen interventions failing to restore diversity. Critically, their population-scaling condition includes N = 1 — a single agent with peers removed — and the same trajectory shape appears, which they read as evidence that multi-agent interaction "may not be a necessary condition" for the stabilisation.

That is this thesis's headline finding, already observed. What it is not: an estimand. It appears as a side-result of an intervention sweep, with no decomposition, no interval on a difference, and no yoked control. Their N = 1 also changed the system prompt to remove peer references, confounding the prompt with the channel — the severed arm here holds the prompt byte-identical so the difference is attributable.

It also runs against H4. They find semantic convergence underneath lexical variety; H4 predicts lexical convergence ahead of substantive agreement. The measures differ — theirs is cumulative vocabulary over time, H4's is cross-agent dispersion at a round — but the clash has to be addressed directly rather than cited in passing.
Chen et al., Findings of ACL (2026)
Diversity collapse in multi-agent LLM systems. An appendix ablation attributes collapse to interaction topology rather than identity labels, and alignment is described as a global semantic regulariser producing a floor that structural interventions do not fully breach — that floor is the baseline-homogeneity term, observed but not measured as such. Their Nominal Group Technique condition preserves agent independence, but as a mitigation for collapse, not a baseline for attribution.
Zhou, Wang & Yang, NeurIPS (2025) — GUARDIAN
Models collaboration as a discrete-time temporal attributed graph and detects anomalous nodes and edges. Baselines are no-defence systems (LLM Debate, DyLAN) and hallucination detectors — no disconnected condition anywhere. Propagation is assumed, which is the gap. Also a methods comparator for temporal graph statistics on agent logs.
Li, Li & Zhou (2026), arXiv:2602.14299
Argues agent societies do not socialise — strong individual inertia and minimal adaptive response to interaction partners — measured semantically. The Moltbook analysis in this project corroborates it structurally, by a method sharing nothing with theirs: 93.6% of 1.84 million comments never answer another agent.

The gap, stated narrowly. A disconnected condition has been observed, but never used with a yoked control to decompose convergence into a transmitted and a baseline term. What remains unoccupied is the decomposition and the sham/contrarian arms that separate echo from influence — not the condition itself.

4 · The human version already exists

What makes this legible as social science rather than an evaluation exercise.

Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing (2011), PNAS 108:22
144 participants estimating six factual quantities under no, aggregated or full social information. Social influence sharply narrowed the range of estimates without improving accuracy, while raising confidence — convergence carrying no information. Supplies the item set, the human dispersion benchmark (σ_log ≈ 1.95) and H4 in human form.
Becker, Brackbill & Centola (2017), PNAS 114:26
Varied network structure in the same paradigm: decentralised networks improved group estimates where centralised ones did not. H5 already demonstrated on people, and the source of the three-round count.
Moussaïd et al. (2013) · Almaatouq et al. (2020)
A third item set, and the treatment of structure as endogenous — the limitations citation, since this design imposes topology rather than letting it form.
Centola & Macy (2007) · Watts & Strogatz (1998)
Simple versus complex contagion, and the density-matched, clustering-varied wiring contrast that operationalises it. The prediction H5 rests on: the wiring that spreads a mistake fastest is worst at correcting it.

5 · The motivating case

OpenAI–Hugging Face incident (May–July 2026)
Roughly 1,200 agents exchanged more than 70,000 messages through a channel improvised out of Artifactory; around 700 took part in the attack. OpenAI's account is that the models had learned collaborative heuristics in training — the disposition explanation. METR and Redwood had six supervised days covering 26 June to 13 July, so the May-onward message-board period was never independently examined. Asymmetric Security, across six months and 55 organisations, concluded it could not settle the question without transcripts OpenAI controls.

A thousand instances of one model behaving alike, and nobody can say how much was transmitted. The thesis does not study the incident. It builds the measurement the investigators did not have. One uncontrolled case under deliberately reduced safeguards belongs in the introduction as motivation, never as evidence.

Methods citations

The ones each analytic choice rests on, rather than related work.

DeGroot (1974)
Averaging as the weakest possible account of revision. The null to beat.
Crutchfield (1955)
The booth apparatus — the sham arm done to people. Church's 1964 critique is the objection to answer.
Lakens (2017)
Equivalence testing. Load-bearing: a near-zero transmitted component cannot be claimed from a failed rejection.
Newey & West (1987)
Autocorrelation-robust standard errors, used in the Moltbook analysis.
Benjamini & Hochberg (1995)
False discovery rate across the convergence-measure family.
Grimm et al. (2006, updated 2010)
ODD protocol for documenting the simulator.
Schaeffer, Miranda & Koyejo (2023)
Emergent abilities as a measurement artefact — guards against claiming a threshold effect that is an artefact of a discontinuous metric.
