The original reasoning, kept for the argument

Three things depend on the list, and none of them is an engineering decision.

1 · The theme enum has to hold identity across industries, not just companies. That is the whole argument of decision 2.4 and the reason risk_theme exists as a node rather than a section code. The current 10 filers — AAPL, CASY, MCK, MDT, MSFT, MU, NKE, NTAP, PH, TTWO — are tech-heavy plus a convenience chain, a drug distributor, a device maker, a footwear brand and an industrial. No bank, no insurer, no energy, no REIT. Those four carry whole risk categories that do not exist in this corpus: credit and capital adequacy, commodity price exposure, catastrophe risk, tenancy concentration. An enum drafted against today's 10 and then run over 233 filings with financials in them sends the new material to unknown, and the one field we did label stops working.

2 · peer is decided entirely by the list. PEER_OF comes from SIC, so the 7 peer questions are answerable only if the corpus holds ≥2 filers per SIC group. Unverified, but worth checking first: I believe the current 10 overlap at 4-digit SIC in exactly one pair — MSFT and TTWO, both 7372 prepackaged software — and that Apple and Microsoft, the pair the bank actually asks about in P3.3, sit in different codes (3571 vs 7372). If that holds, "compare Apple and Microsoft's regulatory risks" yields no peer edge however well everything else works. One EDGAR call per filer settles it — submissions/CIK##########.json carries sic and sicDescription.

3 · The fiscal label rule has a limit the list can trip. Already flagged in the 19 Nov handover: a filer whose year ends in January or February may name it after the year it began, and none in this corpus does. Retailers are exactly the filers that do this — late-January year ends are the norm. If Kelly's list adds retailers, the label rule mislabels a year, silently.

Sequencing — do not block on it. The design of the labelling job does not change: closed enum, explicit unknown, hand-labelled gold set, per-field agreement. What changes is the enum's contents and how the gold set is stratified. So ask Kelly for the list now, draft the theme list against the industry mix rather than the ten names, and sample the gold set stratified by industry rather than at random.

Also worth asking Kelly: is the list final, and was it chosen for coverage or for convenience? A list picked for clean XBRL and large caps will skew the risk vocabulary toward exactly the disclosures that are easiest to extract.

Does it still make sense to put an LLM on this?

Yes — on one field. Not because LLM labelling is a bad instinct, but because this corpus is unusually well structured. SEC filings are machine-tagged by law, so most of what you would normally pay a model to infer is already asserted by the filer:

slot why no model is needed
filer, form EDGAR metadata
concept the filer's own XBRL tag
section, Item level Data's section_types — 25 codes in 7 topics
section, note level inline XBRL. section-vocabulary.md: note boundaries are "in the markup, not in the formatting" — a parser, not a model
period derived from each filer's own filings, #89
is_table Data's table_cells
segment XBRL dimension
peer SIC code in EDGAR metadata

risk_theme is the only slot with nothing to derive it from. But the mechanism already built is not model labelling either — see the theme section below.

What to say to the client, in one line. Agreeing with the ask, and scoping it: "Yes — and on this corpus that means extending a ten-theme map that already exists, with a person approving each new entry. The other eight slots are already asserted by the filer or by Data, and labelling them with a model would replace exact values with approximate ones in the keys we filter on."

The one thing that would change the answer. If Data's note-boundary parser does not land, note-level section loses its deterministic source and becomes a second candidate for labelling. That is a fallback, not the plan — worth asking where that parser stands.

Do not draft a new list. One is built and running. extraction_pipeline/src/risk_factors.py works in three levels:

The three checks, run against the real output

Measured on runs/2026-11_20filings_label-v2/output/risk_categories.csv — 78 category rows, 12 filings, 10 filers.

1 · The map is complete. This is not the bottleneck. 72 mapped, 6 not-a-theme, 0 unmapped. So "have a model propose map entries for unseen headings" solves a problem that does not exist yet. It would only become the job at a much larger corpus with new industries in it.

2 · The binding constraint is grain, and it is severe.

P3.2 — "what cybersecurity risks did Microsoft disclose?" — is answerable for exactly one filer in ten, and it happens to be the one the question names. That is an accident, not coverage. Every other filer folds cyber into business_operations or legal_regulatory. Same for P1.2 and P3.5 on supply chain: no filer writes a supply-chain heading at all, so the category level cannot reach it either.

This changes the recommendation. What "from the sentences" means, concretely.

Today a sentence gets its theme by position: risk_factors.py assigns every Item 1A sentence the theme of the heading above it. So a sentence about a Taiwanese supplier, a sentence about a factory fire and a sentence about hiring all come back as business_operations, because that is what the heading said.

The proposal is one extra label on that same row: ask the model which of the ten themes the sentence is actually about, independently of the heading above it.

scope
field one — risk_theme, as a second column beside the inherited one
vocabulary the ten themes that already exist, plus unknown
unit one sentence
where Item 1A only
how many 5,130 sentences in the 20-filing run, 5,106 of them under a mapped heading

That is the whole job. Not every chunk, not every field — one enum over five thousand sentences in one section. Keep the inherited theme; the model's label goes beside it, so nothing that works today breaks and the two can be compared.

Why it fixes the question. Apple's cyber sentences sit under a heading mapped to business_operations. A sentence-level label puts them under cybersecurity_privacy, and "what cyber risks did Apple disclose" starts working for all ten filers instead of one.

How to measure it — and the trap. The obvious score is agreement with the inherited theme, and that is wrong, because disagreeing with broad headings is the point. Split it:

  • Where the heading is specific — Microsoft's cyber heading, Nike's international heading — the inherited theme is trustworthy. Agreement there is the real score.
  • Where the heading is broad — all nine business_operations headings — measure how much mass moves and to where, then spot-check a sample by hand. Movement is expected; the question is whether it is sensible.

Either way no new hand-labelling is needed to start, which is usually what kills this kind of work before it begins.

3 · Salience exists, is unused — and the corpus cannot run it yet.

Salience here means one thing only: how much space a filer gave a risk. n_units is the number of Item 1A sentences sitting under a category heading. It is populated on all 78 rows — min 0, median 39, max 546. That is the only measure of "attention" a filing makes available, because a filing has no other way of emphasising something than by writing more about it and putting it earlier.

How I would use it. P3.4 asks which risks got more attention than last year. Compare a theme's share of Item 1A between a filer's two consecutive 10-Ks:

  1. Aggregate n_units from heading level to theme level — headings get renamed year to year, themes do not. This is exactly what the shared library is for.
  2. Use share of Item 1A, not raw count. Item 1A length moves a lot year to year, so a raw count rises when the whole section grows and means nothing.
  3. Require a minimum absolute move as well as a share move, so a theme going from 2 sentences to 4 does not report as "doubled".
  4. Report the raw numbers next to the verdict, so a reader can see what drove it.

Agree the definition with Eval before anyone scores it. "More attention" could mean share up, raw count up, moved earlier in the document, or newly present. Those give different answers on the same filing. risk_categories.csv supports share, raw and newly present today; "moved earlier" needs section_order, which exists.

What it does not measure. Space is not severity. A filer that reorganises its headings shifts every count without changing a word of substance. State that limit whenever the number is shown.

⚠️ Blocked on the corpus, not on the code. The run holds one 10-K per filer — AAPL, CASY, MCK, MDT, MSFT, MU, NKE, NTAP, PH, TTWO, one annual filing each. A year-over-year comparison needs two. So P3.1 ("how did risks change from 2024 to 2025"), P3.4 ("more attention than the previous year") and the eval question M06 ("which risk factors appear in FY2023 that were absent in FY2021") cannot be run on this corpus at all — regardless of schema.

That is the third demand in a row that turns out to be corpus composition rather than engineering: peer needs two filers in one SIC group, risk change needs two years per filer, theme grain needs filers who write specific headings. All three go back to Kelly's list.

Is this an ontology change? Mostly no.
New node type No. RiskFactor already exists, decision 2.4. Same ten risk_ids
New edge type No. Claim -[:CONCERNS_RISK]-> RiskFactor already exists
New key No. Nothing is re-keyed
What changes Which claim points at which theme, and where the heading's theme lives

So it is not a Layer 1 amendment. But it does trip a rule Layer 1 already enforces: CONSOLIDATED_INTO ships "only where a filing explicitly supports the mapping", and PEER_OF is "sourced from SIC and stated as such, or it does not go in." An edge states its source. A theme a model assigned is a different source from a theme inherited from a heading, so CONCERNS_RISK has to carry which one it was. That is a property on an existing edge — a recorded decision, not a schema bump.

The free gold set

The 72 mapped categories are ground truth for a sentence labeller: every sentence under a mapped heading already has a theme by inheritance. Train or prompt against the sentences, score against the heading. No new hand-labelling is needed to measure it — which is the part that usually kills this kind of work.

P4.4 — confirmed, and narrower than hoped

Risk categories come from 10 of 10 10-Ks but only 2 of 10 10-Qs (MSFT and MU). So "what risks are new in the latest 10-Q against the last 10-K" is answerable for two filers out of ten. The rest file a 10-Q whose risk section says nothing to extract.

Slot names — should they match the contract keys?

The tradeoff. One vocabulary is cheaper to hold in your head and impossible to drift. Two vocabularies name a resolution step that has to happen somewhere — and when it is unnamed, it fails silently.

This project has already paid for drift once: RENAMED_FILTERS exists because form_type/form, accession/accession_number and period_end/reporting_period_end all happened. That is the argument for collapsing.

The argument against collapsing entirely is that two of the slots are not renames, they are resolutions that can fail:

  • filer → ticker or cik. A question says "Medtronic"; something has to turn that into a key. The Agent team's ticker map overlaps our corpus on MSFT and AAPL only, and the other eight resolve to None with no filter set — which is the mechanism behind "Medtronic answers a Microsoft question." Collapsing the name hides the step that broke.
  • period → fiscal_year plus fiscal_period. "The last three fiscal years" is one slot and two keys, and it is where the arity gap lives.

Proposed rule: a slot keeps its own name only when resolving it can fail. Otherwise it takes the contract key's name. That collapses concept, section, segment, is_table and form to the contract names, leaves filer and period distinct and documented as resolutions, and gives peer and risk_theme the same name on both sides since they need new keys anyway. Nine slot names, two that differ, each for a stated reason.

Who owns it: Layer 3 is question routing, agreed with Agents, W6 then W10. The mapping table belongs there, not in question_bank_slots.py — which should point at it rather than hold a second copy.
